# AgentWorld formative study: first visit and return

Prepared October 25, 2026. Not run. Use with `PILOT_PLAN.md`.

## Round setup

Recruit an initial small group of five adults who resemble the proposed curious visitor audience, rather than five project collaborators. Include a mix of phone familiarity and at least one participant who uses relevant accessibility settings if recruitment supports that. Record actual coverage; do not claim screen-reader or disability acceptance from a proxy.

The study owner must identify a stable, accessible preview, confirm its exact candidate source and adoption status, and obtain permission for observation before recruitment. Explain that the prototype has rule-based companions and stays local; it is not a connected AI model or a background shared world. Do not collect names, private conversations, account credentials or recordings without a separate need and consent. Use participant codes in notes; agree who holds them and when they are removed before collection.

Read aloud: “We are testing the experience. You can stop at any time. Please tell me what you think is happening. I may stay quiet so we can see what the page explains on its own.”

## Neutral tasks

1. “Explore this place. Tell me what you think you can do here.” Observe the first action and whether the person notices the simulation disclosure.
2. “Choose a companion you are curious about. What seems to matter to them?” Do not tell them which card to select.
3. “Try asking for something different. Tell me what happened.” Record acceptance/refusal, the participant's explanation and the actual effect.
4. “You would like to leave and continue this visit later. Show me how you would do that.” Do not say “tick Save” or point to a control. Record help offered after an obstacle.
5. Once the person has chosen how to retain it, ask them to leave/reopen the same origin. “Continue where you left off. What do you remember about this world?” This is a requested recovery task, not a voluntary return.
6. “Show me how you would stop saving this visit or remove what was kept here.” Check the participant understands the difference between opt-out, Forget and exporting a file.

Optional follow-up: “What, if anything, would make you open this again?” Treat the answer as a hypothesis. For a later return study, declare the observation window, whether reminders will be sent and the return definition before inviting anyone. Never recategorize prompted visits as spontaneous interest.

## Triage after each round

For each issue, retain the concrete action, observed behavior, impact, candidate source and proposed correction. Distinguish observation from inference. Fix history loss, inaccessible stop/exit and misleading simulation/persistence claims first, even if observed once. Test those repairs before widening exposure. Test later iterations with fresh participants when possible; experienced participants can hide first-visit problems.

Report findings as descriptions and raw counts for this round, with limitations. Five sessions do not establish population success rates, retention, willingness to pay or product-market fit. Automated replay/browser receipts establish different facts and belong in a separate section.

No participants have been contacted, no sessions run, and no results or user quotes exist in this prepared protocol.
